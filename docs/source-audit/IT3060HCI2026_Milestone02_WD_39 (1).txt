1)Introduction 
1.1) Project Overview 
 
The Sports Equipment Rental and Exchange App is a mobile application designed to make 
sports equipment accessible and affordable for users while providing owners with a safe way 
to rent out unused gear. The main purpose of the system is to provide a simple , trustworthy 
platform where users can search for equipment nearby, check real -time availability, and 
complete bookings without confusion.  
The application serves four main stakeholder user groups: Renters/Borrowers, Private 
Equipment Owners/Lenders, Commercial Rental Shops, and Platform Administrators. Renters 
can search for gear by location, view equipment details and verified owner profiles, select 
rental dates, choose pickup or delivery, and complete secure payments. Private equipmen t 
owners can list unused personal gear, set pricing and availability, review rental requests, and 
handle handovers using condition -verification tools. Commercial rental shops can manage 
larger equipment inventories, set flexible pricing models, and handle high-volume rental 
requests. Administrators monitor platform activities, oversee user verification, and manage 
dispute resolution.  
The design of the application was directly based on the research findings from Milestone 01. 
Research revealed that 65% of potential users avoid sports activities due to high equipment 
costs, while owners hesitate to rent out gear due to deep concerns abou t item damage (62%) 
and hygiene (47%). Furthermore, social proof—such as user ratings and reviews (81%)—and 
photo-backed con dition checks were identified as essential features to build trust between 
strangers.  
1.2) Design Development Process 
For Milestone 02, the interface was developed step by step through an iterative human-centered 
design approach rather than jumping straight to final visuals. First, the team mapped every user 
requirement from Milestone 01 to specific UI components. Multipl e layout variants were 
explored through initial sketches to test different interaction ideas. The strongest concepts were 
converted into wireframes to establish layout structure, which were then linked into a clickable 
low-fidelity prototype. Finally, the design was upgraded into a polished high -fidelity 
interactive prototype in Figma and prepared for end-user testing.  
The overall process followed the Assignment 02 workflow:  
User Research → Requirements → Sketches → Wireframes → Low -Fidelity Prototype 
→ Refinement → High-Fidelity Prototype → User Testing

1.3) Design Objective 
The primary design objective was to create a clean, trustworthy marketplace that removes the 
friction of renting sports gear. Focus was placed on providing clear location -based discovery, 
visible owner credibility badges, transparent pricing, and an effortless handover verification 
flow. Because transacting with strangers involves uncertainty, the interfa ce was purposefully 
designed with simple navigation, prominent trust indicators, and clear photo-confirmation steps 
to help users feel confident and secure throughout the process.

2.3 Interface Traceability Approach 
For this milestone, the requirements were used as the starting point for the interface design. 
Each major interface was linked to one or more requirements so that the final prototype could 
be checked against the problems identified during the research stage.  
For example, FR-01 led to the location -based search and filtering interfaces, while FR -03 led 
to the booking and secure payment checkout screens. Similarly, FR -04 wa s used when 
designing the photo-backed condition check and deposit hold flow so that an owner can safely 
lend gear without fearing undocumented damage. This approach was used to make sure the 
design of GearGo was based on the findings from Milestone 01 rat her than adding screens 
without a clear purpose.

8) User Testing 
8.2 Testing Method 
• Approach: Moderated Usability Testing conducted in-person and remotely via screen 
sharing. 
• Protocol: Concurrent Think-Aloud (CTA), where participants verbalize their thoughts, 
expectations, and hesitations while interacting with the prototype. 
• App / Tool Used:  Interactive Lovable web prototype running on a mobile viewport. 
Prototype 
• Sample Size:  5 participants , representing university student renters, private gear 
owners, and local club players.  
• Procedure: 
1. Brief introductory briefing and consent recording. 
2. User performs 4 structured core tasks sequentially without guidance. 
3. Quantitative logging (time on task, task success rate, error count). 
4. Post-test questionnaire (System Usability Scale — SUS) and brief qualitative feedback 
interview.

8.4 Testing Metrics 
Quantitative and qualitative metrics used to evaluate prototype usability: 
1. Task Completion Rate (TCR):  Percentage of tasks successfully completed without 
critical failure or moderator intervention. 
2. Time on Task (ToT):  Average duration (in seconds) taken to complete each task 
scenario. 
3. Error Frequency: Number of slips, mis clicks, or wrong screen navigations per task. 
4. Single Ease Question (SEQ): Post-task rating on a 1–7 scale (1 = Very Difficult, 7 = 
Very Easy).

9) Usability Issues and Recommendations 
9.1 Identified Usability Issues 
The following usability issues were identified across the 5 participant sessions:  
• UI-01: Ambiguity Between Deposit Hold and Total Billed Amount (Checkout Screen) 
o Observation: On the Confirm Order  screen, participants P2 and P3 expressed 
confusion over the total bill of $320.00. While the price breakdown lists Rental Fee 
($180), Service Fee ($15), Delivery Fee ($25), and Refundable Deposit ($100), users 
initially assumed the $100 deposit was an upfront non-refundable charge rather than an 
authorization hold that gets released upon return.  
o Traceable Requirement: FR-03, FR-04.  
• UI-02: Missing Interactive Cue on the 4 -Angle Photo Verification Guide (Condition 
Check Screen) 
o Observation: During the Condition Check step, the prototype displays 4 thumbnail 
tabs (Front, Back, Left Side, Right Side ) with camera overlay indicators. P3 and P5 
tapped directly on the viewfinder preview rather than clicking the bottom shutter button 
to proceed between angles. The lack of a clear "Next Angle" prompt led to a temporary 
halt in the flow.  
o Traceable Requirement: FR-04.  
• UI-03: Filter Confirmation Dependency (Search / Filter Screen) 
o Observation: In the Filter Equipment  modal, participants adjusted the Distance 
Radius and Price Per Day sliders, but expected the background search list to update in 
real time. Instead, they were required to scroll to the very bottom to tap the primary 
"Apply Filters" button. Two users almost tapped the top "X" close icon, thinking 
changes were auto-applied, which would have discarded their filter selections.  
o Traceable Requirement: FR-01.  
 
• UI-04: Chat Integration Gap from Handover Screen 
o Observation: When viewing the Equipment Handover Checklist, users wanted to ask 
the owner about the exact meetup point at Lincoln Park North Entrance. They had to 
exit back to the main navigation bar to access "Messages" rather than opening an 
immediate chat shortcut directly from the handover card.  
o Traceable Requirement: FR-05.

11)Conclusion 
 
Overall, Milestone 02 successfully transformed the research findings from Milestone 01 into a 
tested and refined interactive prototype for the GearGo Sports Equipment Rental and Exchange 
App. The prototype covered the identified functional and non -functional requirements across 
all four stakeholder groups. Usability testing showed a SUS s core of 79.5 and a 96% task 
completion rate, indicating good overall usability. The testing also helped identify a few areas 
for further refinement, particularly payment/deposit information, listing photo guidance, and 
search-distance adjustment. These fin dings provide a clear direction for the next stage of 
development and demonstrate an evidence -based, human -centered design process.
