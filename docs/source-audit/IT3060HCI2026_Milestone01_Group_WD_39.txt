1.Problem statement and justification for selection  
1.1 Problem statement  
 
Accessing sports equipment can be difficult for people who participate in sports occasionally or 
need equipment only for a short period. Purchasing sports equipment can be expensive and 
inconvenient, while informal methods such as borrowing from friends or  family may not always 
provide the required equipment when needed. The primary user research conducted through 
Google Form indicates that respondents have experienced difficulties related to obtaining suitable 
sports equipment, particularly when equipment is not readily available. The research also 
highlights concerns when obtaining equipment from other people, including possible equipment 
damage, cleanliness and hygiene, payment or fraud risks, and uncertainty about the reliability of 
equipment providers. Respondents also showed interest in features such as location -based 
equipment searching, ratings and reviews, identity verification, deposits or damage protection, 
secure online transactions, communication, and delivery or pickup options. 
The research therefore indicates a user's need for a more convenient and trustworthy way to 
discover, rent, borrow, or exchange sports equipment. From the equipment owner's perspective, 
there is also a need for a safer way to make unused sports equipment available to other users while 
reducing concerns about damage, loss, and unreliable renters. 
Proposed problem scope – The project focuses on a mobile application that allows users to search 
for available sports equipment, view equipment details and condition, identify equipment based 
on location, request rentals or exchanges, communicate with equipment owners, and comp lete 
transactions through a secure process. The application will also support equipment owners in 
listing unused equipment, managing rental or exchange requests, and protecting their equipment 
through suitable trust and safety mechani sms such as ratings, verification, deposits, or damage 
protection. The scope is focused on improving the discovery, rental, and exchange experience 
rather than developing a general sports management platform.

comparison of concerns, pricing expectations, and feature preferences. The "Which best describes 
you?" filter question was used to separate respo nses by stakeholder category during analysis, 
allowing a single instrument to serve primary and secondary stakeholders without requiring

4. Participants and Data Collection Process 
The method involved creating a structured online questionnaire consisting of 14 questions, 
including both single- and multiple-choice questions, open-text questions, and a Likert scale item 
ranging from 1 to 5, and then distributing it to peers and social contacts from 6 to 14 August 
2026. This type of questionnaire was selected since it enables the quick and inexpensive 
gathering of both quantitative data (such as frequency and ratings) and qualitative data (like 
open-text comments on frustration and wish lists) from a wide range of respondents who occupy 
different stakeholder roles, a feature that is appropriate for the exploratory nature of the research 
question – namely, to find out whether people actually experience this problem. A total of 10 
valid responses were obtained (this is more than the minimum required for the assignment).

5. Data Analysis 
5.1 Analysis Method 
Closed-ended questionnaire items were analyzed descriptively using frequencies and percentages (n = 68 valid 
responses out of 69 submissions). The two open-ended questions were analyzed thematically: responses were read 
in full, recurring ideas were coded, and related codes were grouped into the four themes presented in Section 6.3. 
5.2 Findings 
Demand and willingness to participate 
65% of respondents (44/68) said they have avoided trying a sport because buying equipment was too expensive or 
inconvenient. On the supply side, 76% (52/68) said they would "definitely" rent out equipment they own but rarely 
use. Demand for the app itself is strong: 91% (62/68) rated their likelihood of using it a 4 or 5 out of 5. 
 
Concerns and barriers 
62% cited damage to the item and 47% cited hygiene/cleanliness as their top concern renting from a stranger, well 
ahead of payment/fraud risk (15%) or personal safety (12%). The one coach/club -manager respondent flagged all 
three simultaneously — these risks compound for anyone responsible for equipment beyond personal use.

What builds trust 
User ratings/reviews dominate as the trust mechanism respondents rely on most — 81% selected this, more than 
three times the rate of ID verification (28%), ahead of deposit/insurance (18%) and secure in -app payment (18%). 
 
Feature priorities 
Requested features are practical: online payment & booking (59%), damage protection/insurance (53%), and 
location-based search (51%) lead, followed by in-app chat (47%) and delivery (43%). 
 
5.3 Themes 
Theme 1 — Finding the right equipment nearby, at a fair price, is the loudest complaint  
Repeated near-identically across renters, owners, and shop/rental-business respondents: "it's difficult to find the 
equipment I need at a reasonable price." A discovery and pricing-transparency problem shared across the whole 
marketplace. 
Theme 2 — Damage and hygiene define distrust of strangers 
Damage (62%) and hygiene (47%) far outweigh fraud or safety concerns — respondents worry about physical 
condition, not being scammed. 
Theme 3 — Trust is earned through visible reputation, not paperwork 
Ratings/reviews (81%) outweighs every formal verification mechanism combined — a social-proof model of trust. 
Theme 4 — Friction is real, but it isn't a deal-breaker 
Despite clear frustrations, 91% still rated likelihood to use 4 or 5 — these are solvable UX/trust problems, not 
fundamental rejections.

Appendix A – Sports Equipment Owner Interview 
Participant: Sports Equipment Owner, friend of the researcher. Semi-structured interview, 
approximately 1–2 minutes. 
• Owns mainly cricket equipment, including bats, gloves and pads, with some equipment that is 
not used regularly. 
• Has previously lent sports equipment to friends and experienced issues such as late returns and 
equipment not being properly cared for. 
• Main concerns when giving equipment to an unknown person are equipment damage, loss, and 
not receiving the equipment back. 
• Ratings, reviews, identity verification and a security deposit would increase trust when renting 
or exchanging equipment. 
• Equipment listings would help owners make their available equipment visible to potential 
renters. 
• Secure payments and security deposits could provide greater financial protection for equipment 
owners. 
• Booking notifications would help owners stay informed about rental requests and bookings. 
• Owners would benefit from information about equipment condition and rental terms. 
• The platform should encourage accountability for returning equipment on time and in good 
condition.
